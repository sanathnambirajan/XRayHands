X-Ray Hands: Embodied Volumetric Inspection of Hidden Defects in CT Data
Cut, peel, probe, see through and pull pores out of real industrial X-ray CT scans with bare hands, in mixed reality on Meta Quest 3. No menus, no controllers.

Goal

Inspecting industrial X-ray CT (XCT) scans for pores and cracks usually means scrolling 2D slices or orbiting a rendering with mouse and menus; the 3D arrangement of defects must be assembled in the head.

X-Ray Hands places the scanned specimen in the user's real room and makes the bare hands the only instrument: a flat palm is the cutting plane and the cut face shows the true CT slice; two facing palms peel away low-density material; an index finger probes grey values with spatial sound; a pinch-and-pull extracts every segmented defect into a linked 3D scatter plot. This answers Challenge 2, “Beyond Point-and-Click”: each function is an embodied 3D gesture with visible and audible feedback, and there are no floating 2D panels. The aim: find, compare and judge defects (tasks T1–T4) in one workflow linking volume and feature views.

Design Considerations why immersive

  • Spatial structure stays spatial. Stereo and motion parallax show the 3D distribution of thousands of pores directly [1].
  • The hand is the cutting plane. Position and orientation change at the speed of the hand; no slider per axis.
  • Multisensory probe. Grey value as pitch, pore proximity as Geiger clicks at the fingertip, mirrored by a ring pulse.
  • SciVis → InfoVis in one space. Defects fly from the specimen into the chart, keeping object identity [5]; selection is linked both ways [3].
  • Positioning. In-situ AR registers XCT results to a physical sample [6]; we focus on bare-hand manipulation of the volume and linked defect analytics.

Pipeline (Python; segmentation at full resolution)

2Segmentation3³ median; ISO50 threshold (Otsu fails: < 1 % pores, unimodal); hole fill → enclosed air = pores; 26-conn., ≥ 8 vox.
3Featuresvolume, Øeq, sphericity ψ, elongation √(λmax/λmin), centroid, build height; class; TP/FP/FN.
4Unityarea-mean intensity, max-pool labels; gradient + 8³ brick grid baked at load.

28 pytest tests on synthetic shapes (volume ±3 %, ψ ≥ 0.95). Versus the NIST reference (3 176 pores): precision 0.90, recall 0.75, voxel IoU 0.59. Chart z-axis = build height, not depth below surface: an interior tomography has no part surface in the data.

One recognizer serves all gestures: pure-C# detectors output a 0–1 confidence (fuzzy AND of soft thresholds); a hysteresis state machine enters at ≥ 0.95 and exits at ≤ 0.05 after hold times (pinch 2.0 / 3.5 cm, flat palm 150 ms, lens 250 ms, reset 0.6 s); one tool owns the hands at a time. The simulated-hand figure harness found and fixed three conflicts: a peel captured by a running cut, a precision pinch read as grab, a lens threshold below the metal density. Exercised end-to-end in the harness: cut, peel, probe, extract, chart pinch, lens; implemented but not yet exercised: grab, scale, brush, twist, reset. tuning on Quest 3 pending

Risks and mitigation

  • Quest GPU cost per eye → step tiers 96 / 160 / 256, empty-space skipping, ≤ 256³; frame times not yet measured.
  • False positives → hysteresis, hold times, two-hand pre-emption, one tuning asset, regression tests.
  • Data size → crop at full res., then downsample; volumes in Git LFS.
